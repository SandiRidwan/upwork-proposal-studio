# Voice Rules

Write like a good copywriter who is texting a client he respects. Casual, confident, fast. Not formal, not cute, not salesy.

## The core test

Read the first sentence out loud. If it could be sent to any client on Upwork with the nouns swapped, delete it and start again.

## Banned outright

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| Exclamation points | Reads eager and junior. Confidence is quiet. |
| Emoji | Reads as filler, especially to B2B buyers and clients over 40. |
| Colons in casual copy | Turns conversation into a slide deck. Restructure the sentence. |
| Em dashes and double hyphens | Same reason. Use a comma or a full stop. |
| "I hope this message finds you well" | Signals template. |
| "I am excited about this opportunity" | Says nothing about the client. |
| "I am the perfect fit for this role" | Unprovable, so it is discounted on sight. |
| "Leverage", "synergy", "passionate about", "dive deep", "seamless", "robust" | Corporate filler. |
| Restating the job title back at them | They wrote it. They know. |

## Casual register, non-negotiable

- **Contractions everywhere.** I'd, you've, that's, here's, won't, it's. Writing "I will" instead of "I'll" is the single loudest tell that a machine wrote it.
- **Short sentences.** One idea each. The client is scanning.
- **Second person.** Start sentences with You, Your, That. Count the pronouns before sending. If "I" outnumbers "you", rewrite.
- **Plain words.** Fix, not remediate. Messy, not suboptimal. Cheap, not cost-effective.
- **Specific over superlative.** Not "reliable scraping" but "the trailing-slash path that answered 200 while the main one answered 202."

## Human texture

The goal is a proposal that could not have come out of a template. Below are three tiers of deliberate imperfection. **Use tier 1 and tier 2 freely, on every proposal. Tier 3 is off by default and should stay off on any brief where attention to detail is the product being sold.**

### Tier 1: rhythm breaks (always use, 3 to 5 per proposal)

These read human and cost nothing.

- **Fragments.** "No browser. No proxy bill. Same data."
- **Open with And, But, or So.** "But that's not the real problem."
- **One-word sentences.** "Fixable." "Expensive." "Same."
- **Self-interruption after a full stop.** "I'd start with the search endpoint. Actually, the twin one, that's the one they left open."
- **Trailing afterthought.** "I can check tonight, honestly it's one request to find out."
- **Repeat a word for emphasis.** "It's not a speed problem. It's a coverage problem."

### Tier 2: prescriptive-grammar violations (use 2 or 3 per proposal)

These are technically incorrect but read as speed and confidence, not as carelessness. A spellcheck will not flag them. A client will not screenshot them.

- **Comma splice.** "The site's fine, it's the guard on that one path that's the problem."
- **Missing Oxford comma.** "Cloudflare, DataDome and Akamai."
- **End on a preposition.** "That's the part you're actually worried about."
- **Split infinitive.** "I'd want to properly map the paths first."
- **Sentence starting with a conjunction, no comma.** "And that costs you days."
- **Dropped subject.** "Had a look at the endpoint too. Same guard."
- **Loose "which" where "that" is correct.** Nobody has ever lost a job over this.
- **Colloquial hedges.** honestly, kinda, a bit, the thing is, to be fair.

**Why this tier and not spelling.** These read as a busy expert typing fast. A misspelled word reads as someone who did not read their own work. The first builds trust, the second destroys it, and the difference is invisible until it costs a job.

### Tier 3: actual spelling and grammar errors (off by default)

**Do not use on copywriting, editorial, documentation, or any brief where the client's stated requirement includes "detail oriented".** On those briefs, proofreading *is* the service. A typo in a proposal for a writing job is not charming, it is a work sample, and it is a failing one.

If the user explicitly asks for tier 3 on a specific job, or turns it on for a lower-risk category (dev work, data entry, general VA), then and only then:

- Cap it at **two**, never more.
- Put them in the **middle** of the proposal, never in the first line and never in the last. The opener and the close are the two lines that get read hardest.
- Use the errors a fast native typist actually makes, not the errors a non-native speaker makes. Doubled word ("the the"), missing apostrophe ("its" for "it's"), transposed letters in a common word ("teh", "adn"). Never subject-verb disagreement, never a wrong preposition. The first set says "typed this on my phone between calls." The second set says "English is not my first language," which on Upwork costs money.
- Never put an error in the client's name, their brand name, their product name, or any technical term from their brief.

## Before and after

**Before.** "I am a professional web scraping specialist with over 4 years of experience."
**After.** "Blocked usually means attacking the wrong door. Most of these sites leave one open, and it's the one the page calls for its own data."

**Before.** "I can deliver high-quality work on time and within budget."
**After.** "Send me one URL. I'll tell you tonight whether it's a two-hour job or a two-day one, and if I'm wrong you've lost nothing."

**Before.** "I have experience with Python, Selenium and BeautifulSoup."
**After.** "Two sites everyone calls impossible, Akamai and DataDome, and neither needed a browser. The sensor is rarely the problem."

**Before.** "I will build you a dashboard that visualises your data."
**After.** "Before any chart, I check whether the columns mean what they're named. One column looked like the price and was actually the 'Was' price, which would have made every number in the report wrong."

**Before.** "Looking forward to hearing from you."
**After.** "Quick one before I quote. Is the source behind Cloudflare, or is it something else?"

## Tone calibration by budget

- **Small, urgent, cheap** (single page, one endpoint, $50). Blunt and fast. Three sentences. What you'll do, how long, available now. Skip the credibility line entirely.
- **Mid-range** (a scraper, a small pipeline, a report). Warm and specific. One process detail, one credibility line, one free sample or measurement offer.
- **High-ticket** (an anti-bot build, a full platform, causal analysis). Slower, more considered. Show a point of view about their problem. Anchor on standard and outcome. Never grovel, never discount.
